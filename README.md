# Aelzharvex

> A feather fell upward. The sky refused to say what had released it.

## The Hall Above the Sleeping Desert

Aelzharvex is described as a hall suspended above a desert that has never seen daylight. Its pillars rest on nothing visible. Its ceiling is made of pale stone through which distant birds can sometimes be heard walking.

Visitors reach the hall by following a trail of feathers. The feathers do not lie upon the sand; they hover just above it, each pointing toward a different horizon. Those who follow their direction become lost. Those who follow the spaces between them eventually find a staircase descending from the dark.

There are no steps beneath the staircase. The visitor climbs by remembering the weight of something they have already surrendered.

## The Bird That Wore a Dragon's Shadow

At the center of the hall stands a perch of black ivory. A small white bird sits there with its head turned toward a closed curtain. Beneath it, the floor bears the shadow of a winged dragon, complete with horns, folded claws, and an eye that appears to open when nobody looks at the bird.

The bird makes no sound. The shadow occasionally breathes, lifting a thin layer of dust from the floor. In that dust, visitors recognize distant streets, unfamiliar faces, and the shape of a door they have encountered only in childhood dreams.

One visitor tried to touch the shadow. His hand passed through the floor without disturbing it. He withdrew carrying a white feather that cast the shadow of a closed curtain.

The bird turned its head. Behind the curtain, something began to count.

## The Twelve Bowls of Rain

Along the eastern wall stand twelve bowls containing rain. The water does not settle. Each drop remains in the act of falling, held at a different distance above the bottom of its bowl.

The first bowl reflects a storm that has already ended. The last reflects a cloud that has not formed. Between them lie ten skies that the hall's inhabitants refuse to describe.

A traveler poured one bowl into another. Nothing spilled. Both bowls became empty, and the sound of rain filled a room somewhere beyond the curtain. The counting stopped for precisely the length of time required to forget a familiar name.

When the traveler looked again, a thirteenth bowl stood beneath the perch. It contained a single grain of sand, falling toward a bottom that seemed farther away with every breath.

## A Catalogue of Unreliable Appearances

The inhabitants record what visitors see, but no account remains unchanged overnight. A staircase becomes a wound in the ceiling. A feather becomes a small white door. A bowl becomes the outline of a person looking down into water.

The only sentence that survives every alteration describes the curtain as closed. Its color varies, its height varies, and the sound behind it varies. Its state does not.

An inhabitant attempted to remove the sentence. By morning, the catalogue contained only that sentence, repeated in the handwriting of every visitor who had ever entered the hall.

There was one unfamiliar hand. Its letters resembled scratches made by folded claws. The inhabitant preserved the page beneath the perch, where the dragon's shadow kept it warm.

## The Breath Written in Ivory

**Ael — zhar — vex — othuun.**

The sounds are found along the perch, separated by marks resembling small open wings. They can be read only in the reflection of a bowl containing rain. An empty bowl shows the marks but none of the sounds.

The traveler with the feather tried reading them aloud. The bird remained silent. The shadow moved toward the curtain, leaving the perch without any darkness beneath it.

For a moment, everyone present saw the desert through the floor. Far below, a vast creature lay sleeping in the sand. Its wings were open. Upon one wing rested a white bird whose shadow resembled a hall suspended in the dark.

The traveler stopped speaking. The floor became stone again.

## What the Curtain Heard

> Do not ask the bird to choose a sky.
>
> Do not ask the shadow to choose a body.
>
> When the rain begins to fall, leave the smallest bowl untouched.
>
> Something has already placed its name inside it.

These words were heard after the shadow returned to the perch. Nobody could tell whether they came from the curtain, the ivory, or the sleeping creature beneath the floor.

The inhabitants repeated them to later visitors, who occasionally supplied an additional line: if the feather reaches the ceiling, remember which way you entered.

Nobody remembers teaching that line. Nobody has found an entrance when attempting to leave by the same path.

## The Feather Beyond the Ceiling

The hall remains suspended. The bird remains small and white. Twelve bowls preserve their falling rain, while a thirteenth waits beneath the perch with a grain of sand that refuses to arrive.

Sometimes the curtain bulges as though a wing were resting against its other side. Sometimes the distant birds stop walking, and a visitor finds a feather floating near the ceiling.

Aelzharvex offers no direction in that silence. The staircase hangs above the desert, waiting for someone who can remember what they surrendered without remembering to whom.
